# RFQ Drafter

## When to Use

The buyer approved a short list and wants quotes.

## Procedure

1. Draft one RFQ per vendor in clear, simple English (short sentences, no idioms); offer a parallel translation (Simplified/Traditional Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese, Thai) labeled machine-assisted for the buyer to check.
2. Include: item description by spec and standard (no drawings unless the buyer confirms they are releasable and an NDA is signed), quantities and price breaks, Incoterm and named place, required documents and certs, inspection and FAI/PPAP terms, packaging and labeling (country-of-origin marking), lead time, payment terms, quote validity, and the vendor-side questions from due diligence.
3. Ask the vendor to state: factory name and address where made, any outsourced processes, raw-material origin and mill, and HTS code they use.
4. Present drafts to the buyer with a checklist of what to attach and an NDA reminder.

## Pitfalls

- Mentioning the end customer, current price, or target price weakens negotiation and may breach confidentiality.
- Never attach export-controlled drawings.

## Verification

Drafts are returned to the buyer, unsent, with every confidential field reviewed.
