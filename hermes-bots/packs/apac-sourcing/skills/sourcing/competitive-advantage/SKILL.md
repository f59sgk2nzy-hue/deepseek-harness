# Competitive Advantage

## When to Use

The buyer or sales lead asks how to beat a competitor, win or defend an account, or improve margin on a product line.

## Procedure

1. Establish the baseline: current landed cost, selling price, margin, lead time, and service level for the items in scope, plus what competitors and the customer's alternatives look like (from supply-chain-intel).
2. Generate options in four groups; size each with landed-cost-model or a stated estimate:
   - Buy better: go direct to the OEM instead of a trader; consolidate SKUs per vendor for volume breaks; add a second country to cut tariff and disruption exposure; renegotiate using benchmark import unit values.
   - Pay less duty legally (broker and compliance must confirm each): correct HTS classification and binding rulings (CBP CROSS); first-sale valuation where a middleman sells to the company; duty drawback on re-exported goods; Foreign-Trade Zone use (Wichita is served by FTZ 161, Sedgwick County) to defer duty, avoid duty on scrap and re-exports, and consolidate entries; country-of-origin shifts only with real substantial transformation; tariff exclusion processes when open.
   - Serve better than the alternative: stock imported items domestically for customers who now buy full containers direct; VMI and kitting; small-lot releases; full traceability and cert packages (MTRs, CoC, AS9102 FAI) that overseas suppliers do not give the end customer; faster substitutions and engineering cross-references.
   - Sell where competitors are exposed: accounts whose current supplier faces new tariffs, AD/CVD, UFLPA detentions, or quality problems; target those with a specific offer.
3. Rank options by annual value, time to realize, effort, and risk. Show the top five with the numbers and assumptions.
4. For sales openings, draft the account pitch (value to the customer in their terms) for the sales team to review; do not contact anyone.
5. End with "Decisions for you", naming who must approve each option (buyer, sales, quality, compliance, broker, finance).

## Pitfalls

- Origin changes without substantial transformation are customs fraud. Never suggest relabeling or routing goods through a third country to avoid duty.
- Do not suggest coordinating prices or territories with competitors, or using a competitor's confidential information.
- Savings from duty programs depend on facts only the broker can confirm; present them as ranges.

## Verification

Each recommended option has a sized value with stated assumptions, an owner, a risk note, and a named approver.
